## Recommendation and limits

Retain **Option A as the first candidate to prove**, not a selected production provider: an approved OIDC identity service resolves the family identifier and verifies its credential, then a documented Supabase Auth OAuth callback creates a genuine Supabase session. Native staff login stays within Supabase Auth. The school form fields may be hosted on the identity service after redirect; preserving fields does not imply an embedded Next.js credential form. Do not send a PIN in authorization URL parameters or attempt a resource-owner-password exchange to the IdP.

Keycloak is an investigatory candidate because it documents custom authentication extensions; it is not a verified turnkey school PIN solution. Its internal SPI upgrade risk, credential storage, guardian alias lookup, reset, denial behavior and operating burden require review. No provider/account was created. See [comparison](AUTH-OPTIONS-COMPARISON.md), [sequences](STUDENT-PARENT-LOGIN-SEQUENCES.md), [trust boundaries](SESSION-RLS-TRUST-BOUNDARIES.md) and [decision gate](STEP-9-DECISION-GATE.md).

## Findings requiring clarification of Step 8

1. Automatic same-email identity linking is documented, so a design must actively prevent Student/Parent/staff cross-linking; “do not auto-link” in app code alone is insufficient. Investigate family providers without email and disabled manual linking, but prove actual behavior before adoption. Do not assert that these settings eliminate every linking path.
2. The former 10/12-digit proposal is not a production-approved security threshold. For the remote single-factor interpretation, evaluate at least a 15-digit randomly generated numeric secret as the new candidate, with usability review and the limitations in [PIN policy](PIN-SECURITY-POLICY.md). Shorter PINs require a separately approved stronger-factor design, not a renamed local-device exception.
3. Both documented federation primitives exist, but no public evidence proves this school's entire preprovisioned PIN-to-session flow. No native arbitrary-registration/PIN exchange was established. A before-create hook is a possible control, not proof of exact upstream subject approval.

These findings justify narrowly updating the earlier PIN design and authentication plan. Historical reports remain unchanged. Readiness is conditional until the concrete evidence in the gate exists.
